The Seven Continents Explained (and Why Some Maps Show Six)

In much of the English-speaking world, schoolchildren learn that Earth has seven continents. But "continent" is a convention rather than a precise scientific term, and different countries teach different models. If you track the continents you have visited, it helps to understand what is being counted.

The seven-continent model

The most common model lists these seven continents:

  1. Africa
  2. Antarctica
  3. Asia
  4. Australia (often called Oceania when it includes nearby Pacific islands)
  5. Europe
  6. North America
  7. South America

Other models

Some regions teach six continents or even five. The usual variations are:

  • Eurasia: treating Europe and Asia as a single landmass, since there is no sea between them.
  • The Americas: treating North and South America as one continent, which is the model taught in parts of Latin America and elsewhere.
  • Oceania: using this name for the region that includes Australia, New Zealand and the Pacific islands, rather than "Australia" alone.

None of these is wrong. They reflect different traditions about where to draw boundaries.

Countries that cross continents

A few countries sit across a continental boundary. Russia and Turkey both span Europe and Asia, and Kazakhstan also reaches into Europe. Egypt is mostly in Africa but includes the Sinai Peninsula, which is geographically in Asia. For counting purposes, most travel trackers assign each country to a single continent, usually where most of its land or its capital lies, which keeps the system simple.

Antarctica is different

Antarctica has no countries and no permanent resident population, only research stations. Visitors typically travel on expedition cruise ships, and many of those voyages depart from the southern tip of South America. For many travelers, it is the last continent on their list because of its cost and logistics.

Choosing your own counting rule

If you track continents, write down your rules first:

  • Which model are you using (seven, six, or another)?
  • How do you assign countries that span two continents?
  • Does setting foot in a place count, or do you need to stay overnight?

The same principle applies as with countries: decide in advance, and apply the rule consistently. Travel apps do this by assigning each country to one continent, which is easy to understand even if it is a simplification.

Why it matters

Continent counts are less about geography than about motivation. Aiming to see every continent nudges you toward places you might not otherwise choose, and that is usually where the most memorable trips happen.
