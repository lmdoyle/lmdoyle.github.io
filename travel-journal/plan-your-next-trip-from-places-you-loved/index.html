Plan Your Next Trip Starting From the Places You Loved

Many people plan trips by scrolling through lists of famous destinations. A more reliable approach starts with what you already know: the places you have loved. This guide shows how to turn your travel history into a shortlist of new destinations you are likely to enjoy.

Step 1: List your favorites

Write down your five best trips or places, using your travel journal, photos or ratings if you have them. Do not overthink it. Choose the ones you still talk about.

Step 2: Find what they have in common

For each favorite, note a few traits:

  • Setting: beach, mountains, old city, countryside, a big modern city.
  • Pace: relaxed with long meals, or packed with activities.
  • Weather: warm, mild, snowy.
  • Focus: food, history, nature, nightlife, art, outdoor adventure.
  • Crowds: busy and energetic, or quiet and remote.

Patterns show up quickly. You may realize that four of your five favorites were walkable old towns with great food, which is more useful than "I like Europe."

Step 3: Do the same for your disappointments

Low-rated places are just as informative. If you were underwhelmed by two big resort destinations, that is a hint to avoid similar trips even if they are popular. Knowing what you dislike narrows the field.

Step 4: Look for "similar but new"

With your traits in mind, search for places that share them but that you have not visited. Useful ways to find them:

  • Look at neighboring regions or countries of a place you loved.
  • Search for the trait, for example "walkable historic towns with great food", rather than a destination name.
  • Ask friends who have similar tastes where they would go.
  • Use a recommendation tool, including the suggestions in some travel apps, as a source of ideas.

Step 5: Check the practicalities

A great match still has to work in practice. Compare each candidate on:

  • Time: how many days it realistically needs, including travel.
  • Season: whether your available dates are a good time to go.
  • Budget: flights, accommodation and daily costs.
  • Entry requirements: check official government sources for current visa and health rules before booking.

Step 6: Narrow to three, then choose

Cut your list to three finalists and write one sentence on why each fits. Often the choice becomes obvious, or you realize that two of them could be combined into a single longer trip.

Step 7: Leave room for the unplanned

Your favorites were probably memorable partly because of unplanned moments. Build slack into the itinerary: a free afternoon, a day without reservations. Planning from your past enjoyment is about raising your odds, not scripting every hour.
